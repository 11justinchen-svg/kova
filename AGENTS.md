# Kova artistic operating system

This repository should feel authored, not assembled. Build Kova as an evolving series of digital posters: culturally aware, visually forceful, technically disciplined, and unmistakably intentional.

## Required local skill

For any interface, art direction, landing page, portfolio, campaign, hero, typography, or motion task, load and follow `.agents/skills/kova-scroll-art-direction/SKILL.md`. Inspect its full `assets/reference-*.png` set and read only the referenced guidance needed for the task.

## Creative stance

- Begin with a concept sentence and a visual tension before choosing components.
- Prefer a memorable visual act over a safe collection of patterns.
- Use asymmetry, crop, scale, rotation, collision, and negative space deliberately.
- Make one element dominant in each viewport; allow other elements to support it.
- Preserve an underlying grid even when the surface feels unruly.
- Treat typography as material and photography as narrative, not decoration.
- Create scale contrast with one monumental display voice, one compact reading voice, and at most one metadata mono; follow the skill's `references/typography.md` pairings.
- Let fluorescent color occupy real territory. Do not reduce the identity to an accent button.
- Keep interfaces useful. Artistic friction may slow looking, but must not block understanding or action.

## Anti-generic mandate

Do not default to centered SaaS heroes, rounded feature-card grids, pastel gradient haze, glassmorphism, interchangeable bento layouts, pill overload, decorative blobs, uniform fade-ups, or stock “AI product” imagery. Avoid polite symmetry when the concept calls for tension. Avoid random weirdness when a clear system would be stronger.

## Motion rules

- State the interaction model, trigger, states, transition, and reduced-motion equivalent before implementation.
- Tie motion to meaning: reveal, compare, index, transition, or orient.
- Build one continuous hero gesture plus a small supporting motion vocabulary.
- Favor transforms, opacity, masks, and normalized progress; avoid layout thrash and animation clutter.
- Design reduced motion as a complete static composition with all information present.
- Preserve native touch behavior and keyboard access.

## Reference practice

- Research visual precedents when a task asks for a new direction; record links and describe the mechanism being borrowed.
- Synthesize multiple references. Never clone a full layout or reuse unlicensed imagery.
- Prefer actual brand/product material. If assets are missing, make the intended crop, subject, texture, and licensing requirement explicit.
- Use the supplied poster as the north star for intensity and composition, not as a screen to trace.

## Quality bar

- Recompose at mobile, tablet, and desktop widths; do not merely shrink the desktop layout.
- Check the opening, middle, and closing scroll states, including reverse scroll and resize.
- Verify legibility, contrast, focus visibility, pointer targets, reduced motion, and the absence of accidental overflow.
- Keep technical performance invisible: motion should remain smooth on a mid-range phone.
- Remove any element that lacks a content, navigation, pacing, or conceptual job.

When choosing between “tasteful but forgettable” and “clear with a point of view,” choose the latter.
