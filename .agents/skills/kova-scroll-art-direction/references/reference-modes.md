# Supplied reference modes

Choose one primary mode per page. Borrow at most one secondary mechanism from another mode so the result feels authored rather than moodboard-like.

## Asset map

- `assets/reference-poster.png` — acid fashion archive.
- `assets/reference-cyber-botanical.png` — cyber-botanical terminal.
- `assets/reference-eco-collage.png` — photocopied field journal.
- `assets/reference-tokyo-travel.png` — retro travel monument.

## Shared grammar

All four references rely on one dominant image, extreme large/small typography contrast, a narrow palette, edge-aligned metadata, controlled cropping, print texture, and a layout that behaves like a poster before it behaves like a component library.

## Mode A — acid fashion archive

- Use fluorescent yellow, black, skin tones, and translucent plastic.
- Reveal portrait fragments through large organic masks.
- Rotate a compressed statement and counter it with issue numbers, rules, and a barcode.
- Choreograph the page as aperture → split crop → catalog state → clean release.

## Mode B — cyber-botanical terminal

The flower reference uses a near-black field, electric signal red, cyan/red channel separation, a single monumental flower, large CJK glyphs, dense diagnostic microtype, nested borders, and barcode/label modules.

- Center one organic specimen while machine notation presses in from every edge.
- Use signal red for title glyphs and rules; use cyan only as a spectral image offset.
- Animate a scanner pass, brief channel split, or data-rail activation. Do not loop constant glitch.
- Use real Japanese language only when content warrants it and have it reviewed by a fluent reader.
- Keep the flower or product natural enough to preserve the organic-versus-machine tension.

## Mode C — photocopied field journal

The nature reference uses warm white paper, black ink bleed, lowercase neo-grotesk type, stepped mosaic image masks, muted forest photography, tiny marginal essays, crosshairs, and sparse four-point stars.

- Let the image assemble in irregular rectangular tiles, then settle into stillness.
- Keep large words blunt and simple; create character through photocopy texture and crop rather than a novelty font.
- Place small observations in margins and negative spaces, never over the image unless contrast is protected.
- Use paper grain globally at low opacity, but keep live body text crisp.
- Favor quiet scroll pacing, slow tile reveals, and occasional page-like wipes.

## Mode D — retro travel monument

The Tokyo reference uses cream stock, dusty cyan display type, rust-red architecture, a central vertical landmark, Japanese titling, a small chapter number, and a restrained explanatory paragraph.

- Put the landmark on the central axis and let it pierce or occlude the giant title.
- Keep the background quiet and the title wide, tall, and cropped.
- Use a slow vertical image reveal or pinned title while the landmark advances through it.
- Apply subtle print fade, misregistration, and paper grain; avoid heavy VHS effects.
- Keep travel information accurate and avoid decorative pseudo-Japanese copy.

## Combining modes

Good combinations:

- Acid mask geometry with eco paper texture.
- Cyber diagnostic rails with the Tokyo landmark axis.
- Eco tile assembly with acid-scale type.
- Tokyo palette with Swiss-punk metadata.

Avoid combining fluorescent yellow, signal red, cyan channel split, paper grain, chrome, and multiple mask systems on one screen. Pick a dominant material world.
